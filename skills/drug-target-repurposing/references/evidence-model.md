# Evidence model

## Hard gates

- target and compound identity resolved
- mechanism direction known or explicitly unknown
- relevant tissue/cell exposure plausible
- no disqualifying safety or contraindication signal for the intended population
- evidence licensing and provenance recorded

## Recommended evidence classes

Score each class independently: human genetics, molecular causality, disease context, perturbation/rescue, pharmacology, clinical evidence, safety, and tractability. Record confidence, direction, independence group, and source. Pre-register class weights and minimum advancement rules. Use ordinal tiers with a written rationale instead of opaque decimal precision: Tier 1 requires directionally aligned human genetics plus relevant molecular/context evidence and acceptable safety; Tier 2 lacks one decisive independent class; Tier 3 is hypothesis-generating or has unresolved direction; Excluded fails a hard gate. Project protocols may tighten these definitions but must not relax them after ranks are seen.

## Safety structure

Assess target-organ toxicity, dose and exposure margin, route, duration, relevant population, age/pregnancy constraints, renal/hepatic handling, QT or other mechanism-specific risk, drug-drug interactions, contraindications, class effects, and reasons for prior clinical failure. Separate on-target from off-target risk when possible.

## Contradictions

Retain contradictory results. Distinguish context dependence from irreproducibility. A genetic proxy for lifelong modest modulation is not automatically equivalent to short-term high-dose pharmacological inhibition.

## Validation ladder

Prioritize orthogonal confirmation, relevant primary cells or organoids, dose-response, target engagement, rescue, biomarker response, toxicity, and in vivo or clinical validation as appropriate.
